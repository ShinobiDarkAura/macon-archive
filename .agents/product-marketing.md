## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Small cast-bronze totems and jewellery whose forms are deliberately
ambiguous, so the person holding one decides what it is.

**What it does:** Alex and Hannah design and cast small bronze objects — animals you
can almost name, forms you can't quite place — plus rings and jewellery in bronze and
silver. Hannah carves each in wax, it is lost-wax cast at a foundry in Los Angeles,
then she files the whole surface by hand and patinates it. Every piece carries a name
and a true origin story, and arrives documented like something excavated rather than
bought.

**Product category:** Collectible design objects / art objects. Adjacent to fine
jewellery, designer toys, and memorial-commemorative commissions — but the shelf
customers actually find them on is "meaningful object", not "jewellery".

**Product type:** Made-to-order physical goods, plus a bespoke commission service.

**Business model:** Direct sale through studiomacon.co and Instagram DMs.
- Totems roughly $220–240
- Custom commissions $925 (about 8 hours of bench time)
- Materials $30–50 per piece; a regular totem is about 1 hour of bench time
- Trailing-twelve-month revenue in the $25k–$100k band
- **No discounting, ever.** No sales, no coupon codes, no BFCM markdowns.

## Target Audience

**Measured audience (Instagram, quarter to 11 Aug 2026):** 2,051 followers, +26.3%
on the prior quarter. 35–44 is the largest cohort at 32.9%, then 25–34 at 26%.
**50.9% men / 49% women** — genuinely unusual for a jewellery-adjacent business, and
a signal the studio should take seriously rather than correct for. US 73.9%, UK 6.7%,
France 4.8%.

**Measured buyers:** 94+ collectors, all with email on file. Average LTV $433 against
a **median of $208** — the mean is carried by a handful of deep relationships, not
broad repeat buying. One collector alone is 25% of all revenue; the top 13 are 50%.
Only 13% have bought twice.

**Jobs to be done** (inference, pending the customer-voice research):
1. *Give me something to hold that means something* — a talisman, a worry object, a
   thing on the desk that carries weight beyond decoration.
2. *Help me mark something that mattered* — a person, a pet, a moment, a loss. This is
   the commission job, and the studio's stated growth priority.
3. *Let me give a gift for someone impossible to buy for* — where a generic luxury
   object would read as thoughtless.
4. *Let me collect something with a world behind it* — named pieces, lore, provenance
   documents, a set that is never quite complete.

**Anti-persona:** anyone shopping on price, anyone who needs to be told exactly what
the object is before they can want it, and anyone for whom "handmade" means "cheaper
than the branded equivalent".

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem the object solves:** most objects people own mean nothing. The things
that do carry meaning are usually inherited, not chosen. There is very little you can
buy at $200–900 that is both beautiful and *personally significant* — luxury goods
signal money, mass décor signals taste, and neither holds a story that belongs to you.

**Why the alternatives fall short:**
- Fine jewellery signals value, not meaning, and is priced by material.
- Mass-produced décor is interchangeable and everyone recognises it.
- Fine art is priced out of reach and can't be carried in a pocket.
- Memorial products are usually grim, literal, and badly designed.

**Emotional tension:** wanting to honour something — a person, a pet, a chapter —
and finding that everything available is either too literal, too sentimental, or
too obviously bought.

## Competitive Landscape

**Direct:** independent bronze and ceramic object studios; art-toy and designer-object
makers. Fall short mainly on story — most sell form without provenance.

**Secondary:** fine jewellery brands with a narrative bent (Alighieri, Sarah &
Sebastian). Fall short because the object is worn and social rather than held and
private.

**Indirect:** doing nothing, or a framed photograph. Falls short because it isn't
tactile, and the whole mechanic here is holding.

## Differentiation

**Key differentiators:**
- **Ambiguity as the product.** The forms read as a bear, a hare, a camel without a
  single explicit feature. The owner completes the meaning. *"We discover by holding."*
- **True origins, never invented.** Pip is Hannah's grandfather's cast-iron piggy
  bank. Lunar Canyon and Milo come from Milos, Greece, on their honeymoon. Yaël is the
  Hebrew name Hannah chose when converting to Judaism with Alex and Alex's father, a
  cantor. Ida's Wonder Horn is from Sendak's *Outside Over There*. Ori began as a
  commission for a friend who loved bears and became a bestseller.
- **Ancient meets futurism.** A consistent aesthetic axis — Dune is a deliberate and
  constant influence — that almost nobody else in the category occupies.
- **The Bureau of Provenance.** Pieces arrive documented, archived, and accounted for.
  The conceit is real infrastructure, not a tagline.
- **Two people, named and present.** Hannah carves; Alex writes, shoots and designs.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "What actually is it?" | That's the point, and it's worth saying out loud rather than apologising for. It becomes whatever you decide it is. |
| "$925 for a custom feels like a lot." | Eight hours of hand work, carved in wax, cast in bronze, filed by hand. Nothing to decide from a drawing — the design comes first, free. |
| "I don't know where I'd put it." | Owners keep them on desks, shelves, stacks of books, in a pocket. It's meant to be picked up. |
| "Is bronze worth it versus silver/gold?" | Bronze is the material of artifacts. It ages, marks, and takes on the hand that holds it. |

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** an occasion that demands more than a bought thing — a death, a birth, a
wedding, a move, a pet, an anniversary.
**Pull:** the forms themselves, the origin stories, and the sense of a world with more
in it than one object.
**Habit:** defaulting to flowers, gift cards, or a framed photo.
**Anxiety:** "will it feel cheap in person", "will they understand what it is",
"is it worth this much for something small".

## Customer Language

**How they describe the pieces (verbatim, published):**
> "You created it with something in your head, a story, and that means something more
> than just its material form." — Laurel, NY, owns three pieces

> "I love the shape of Ren. I have it on a stack of books, it's sitting right on top
> so I can see it. As I look across the room, I love the elegance of the form, the
> kind of mid-century, kind of Swedish feel of it, it's gorgeous." — collector,
> Pacific Northwest

Note what both do: they talk about **where it lives** and **what it means**, not
craftsmanship, material, or price. *(A fuller verbatim vocabulary is being mined from
the collector archive; this section should be rewritten from that evidence, and it is
currently the single largest gap in the business's self-knowledge — `why` is blank on
all 94 collector rows.)*

**Words to use:** hold, carry, live with, ends up, keeps, artifact, form, provenance,
archive, excavated, marked, honour, discover.

**Words to avoid:** *luxury, curated, elevate, must-have, treat yourself, shop now,
limited time, don't miss out, exclusive, drop* (as a verb), *investment piece*, any
mention of AI, and em dashes (house style joins short ideas with commas). Nothing
pitchy. Nothing that would embarrass either of them if a collector read it aloud.

**Glossary:**
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Totem | A small cast-bronze object, the studio's core product |
| Bureau of Provenance | The studio's archival conceit and its internal system |
| Artifact | How a piece is framed: found rather than manufactured |
| Keeper | Alex or Hannah, in the Bureau's own language |

## Brand Voice

**Tone:** warm, plain, unhurried. Writes like a person, never like marketing.
**Style:** short ideas joined with commas rather than stacked. No em dashes. Nothing
pitchy. Each message ends on something answerable in a sentence.
**Personality:** archival, ancient-futurist, quietly funny, generous, uncommercial.

## Goals

**Business goal:** replace both founders' incomes within a year — *while* learning why
people actually buy, so what gets built is durable rather than a good year. When the
two conflict, weight toward **why**.

**Key conversion action:** two, in order of value —
1. A custom commission enquiry (currently begins as a DM, $925)
2. A first totem purchase, which is the entry into the collection

**Binding constraint:** **demand.** Bench capacity is available. Recommendations
should weight acquisition, offer and messaging over efficiency and throughput.

**Current metrics:** $40,747 lifetime collector revenue across 94 collectors; ~$180/mo
ad spend at 1.69× blended ROAS; 2,051 Instagram followers growing 26% per quarter;
13% repeat rate; 94 collector emails on file that have never been mailed.

**Open constraint on channels:** no wholesale by default, with one stated exception —
a museum gift shop with heavy foot traffic and real exposure would be worth doing.
